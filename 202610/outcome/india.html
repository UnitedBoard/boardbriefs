Board Briefs
 
October 2026
 
 
 
 
 
India Once Again
 
 

In late August, the United Board returned to India to continue and amplify the engagement work done during the June Study Trip. Over the course of a single week, two staff members led faculty development workshops in Assam, Pareena visited a longstanding partner in Jabalpur, and the United Board joined the All India Association for Christian Higher Education (AIACHE) in convening a three-day workshop in New Delhi for academic leaders of Christian colleges from across India.

From August 24 to 27, David and Anita traveled to Assam in Northeast India to conduct faculty development workshops for two institutions new to the United Board — St. Ann's College, Kokrajhar and Don Bosco College Bongaigon. The workshops marked an early step in building relationships with these colleges.

 
 

On August 27, Pareena and Sandra visited St. Aloysius College (Autonomous), Jabalpur, founded in 1951 and now serving about 6,000 students with 314 faculty members. Pareena was invited as Chief Guest at the Gold Medal Award Ceremony held as part of the college’s 75th-year jubilee, presided over by Bishop Dr. Fr. G. Vazhan Arasu of the Catholic Diocese of Jabalpur. Addressing the medal winners, she encouraged the students, particularly the young women, to see themselves as future leaders, to step beyond their comfort zones, and to recognize that achievement is always supported by parents, teachers, and mentors.

A session with faculty who have taken part in United Board programs revealed the depth of a partnership that began in 2016. More than 30 faculty members have participated in United Board programs, and the college has built on that experience to create its own faculty development framework. Most striking was the multiplier effect: United Board–supported faculty have extended training to 60 schools in and around Jabalpur, reaching more than 500 schoolteachers, several of whom spoke about how it had changed their own teaching. Participants asked whether the United Board’s online programs, particularly those on AI, could be opened to institutions beyond its network, and Pareena agreed to explore the possibility.
 
 

Speaking to the full faculty body on “Faculty as Change Agents in a Time of Seismic Shift,” Pareena framed India’s National Education Policy 2020 as placing faculty at the center of transformation, as facilitators of deep learning rather than deliverers of content. “Skills without values create workers,” she observed, “and values without skills create frustration.” In a closing dialogue, students spoke of their wish for more experiential learning, internships, entrepreneurship, and global exposure, of the competencies they will need in an age of AI, and of the need for regular counseling and mental health support.
 
 

The visit to India culminated in the AIACHE–United Board Workshop on Academic Leadership and Innovation, held in New Delhi from August 28 to 30. The workshop brought together principals, vice principals, deans, quality assurance coordinators, and senior faculty from Christian institutions across India. Its three days followed a deliberate progression: “Lead with Clarity,” “Renew What We Do,” and “Turn Learning into Action.”

Welcomed by Rev. Fr. Dr. Xavier Vedam, SJ, General Secretary of AIACHE, Pareena delivered the presidential address. She urged leaders not to measure excellence only through rankings, accreditation, and placements, but to ask three fundamental questions: What is education for? What kind of institution are we called to become? Whom do we serve? She named foresight and fortitude as the twin qualities leaders need today, and described innovation as something broader than technology: redesigning curricula around actual students, connecting learning to local problems, and accomplishing more through partnership.

 
 

Over the following sessions, participants worked through institutional and departmental development planning, curriculum renewal, and pedagogy and assessment, as well as building cultures of research and innovation. Sandra led a session on building research culture, moving from service to scholarship and societal transformation.

On the final day, David and Anita facilitated two sessions on student engagement and leadership, urging institutions to prepare students “for their future, not our past” and to create real channels for student voice. Participants then named what they would carry home and the next step they would take to put it into practice.

 
 

Taken together, the August visits carried forward the themes heard during the June study trip: the urgency of AI readiness, the importance of listening to students, and the value of whole person education in a rapidly changing landscape. They also showed the United Board’s investment in people bearing fruit well beyond its original participants, from the classrooms of Jabalpur to a national gathering of academic leaders in New Delhi.
