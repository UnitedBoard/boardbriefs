Board Briefs
 
August 2026
 
 
 

Dear Trustees,

As another season draws to a close, I am grateful for your continued guidance and am delighted to share this edition of Board Briefs. These pages record not simply a very busy summer, but one in which I saw our network becoming more deeply connected, across institutions, across countries, and across generations of leaders.

We begin in India. From the reception in Chennai to the narrow roads leading to Darjeeling, the June study trip asked a lot of each of us—long days, a demanding schedule, and constant movement across institutions and regions —but, judging from your survey responses, it was well worth the effort! What stays with me is not the itinerary but the quality of what we encountered: the friendships and unstinting hospitality of the four colleges across the six campuses we visited and the warm farewell to our long-serving trustee Paul Appasamy. The photographs that follow capture some of what we experienced. Your own reflections, shared at the end of the section, capture the experiences even more.

I am delighted to share that next year our study trip will take us to Taiwan.

IASACT, a regular summer feature for the United Board, was held for the first time at Payap University. Scholars from across Asia spent a fortnight reflecting on "Theologies of Aging: Interfaith Wisdom for Flourishing in Later Life." Drawing from the deep wells of Christian, Muslim, Buddhist, Hindu, and Confucian faith traditions, participants moved beyond the familiar framing of seeing “later life” as decline, reframing and exploring it instead as a flourishing season of meaning, agency, and moral responsibility. As our societies grow older, this is work the world needs to hear about and where Asian voices have much to say. The gathering also paused to remember the late Dr. Timothy Light, whose imprint on IASACT endures.

In Singapore, our second Heads of Institution Leadership Program convened presidents, rectors, principals, and vice-chancellors around a single question: what is required to lead the next decade of Christian higher education in Asia? They worked through topics such as the future of work, artificial intelligence, and higher education’s fragile business model. They also wrestled with questions related to climate risk. But more than talk, they chose to do something about it. Twenty-one heads of institutions signed a Declaration on Sustainable and Climate-Responsive Campuses. Two institutions, one from Indonesia and the other from South Korea, began conversations then and there and entered into a memorandum of understanding for further collaboration.

It was by design that the 2026–27 Fellows Program opened its Summer Leadership Institute days later, in the same city, illustrating how current heads of institutions and emerging leaders now move along one continuous pathway—illuminating a network that develops leadership at every level rather than at the top alone. This year's Fellows Program is focused on adapting to disruption; I have every confidence the Fellows will collectively develop imaginative solutions that will benefit their institutions.

Elsewhere in this issue you will read about a workshop designed to explore educational needs for tribal and rural schools. This was born from a connection between partners in Meghalaya, India and Thailand that we were pleased to facilitate; a faculty program on leadership in the age of AI, hosted in Hyderabad; and a new memorandum of understanding between Miriam College and Lady Doak College, the fourth MOU signed among our partners this year. While individually modest, together they represent something we have long hoped for: a network that increasingly connects itself.

Our work depends on thoughtful leadership, faithful service, and the trust that joins us together throughout our community. These examples reminded me again that the United Board’s strength is not in the programs we offer, but in the relationships of trust, imagination, and courage that make them possible.

As we begin a new fiscal year, I am delighted to welcome Dr. Alfred Devaprasad to our Board, whose governance experience across Indian education will be a great asset to us. My thanks to the trustees who are stepping into new roles as Secretary, committee chairs, and vice chairs; their willingness to serve is the quiet engine of the United Board. And hearty congratulations to Jerusha Alice J. and Praveena Elizabeth D. of our Chennai office on their well-deserved promotion and new titles, which reflect both their distinguished performance and the increasingly important roles they now play.

Thank you for the wisdom, generosity, and care you bring to our shared mission. I hope the stories that follow offer a glimpse of what your leadership makes possible and of the lives, institutions, and communities being strengthened across our network.

With deep appreciation and warmest regards,

 
 

Pareena Gupta Lawrence
President
United Board for Christian Higher Education in Asia

New Trustee
Alfred Devaprasad

An education administrator with extensive leadership experience across the K–12 and higher education sectors, Dr. Alfred Devaprasad is CEO of Christwood School, Chennai, an institution he helped found, and previously served as President and CEO of the Alpha Group of Educational Institutions. He is Vice Chair of Kodaikanal International School and serves on the boards of Madras Christian College and Schools, Stella Maris College, and several other educational institutions in India. Dr. Devaprasad brings deep governance experience to the United Board, gained across educational and nonprofit organizations in India and abroad.
